Pricing is where most agencies hide their economics. The MRVIN100 model does the opposite: it publishes the framework, so the numbers are the proof of the model.

## What is standardized, what is not

The model makes a clear distinction:

- **The framework is standardized and universal.** Percentages, roles, guarantees and criteria are published once and apply everywhere. Examples: the 40% developer share, the 8 to 12% SDM range, the 72-hour replacement standard.
- **Absolute prices are maintained per market zone.** What is standard in Central Africa can differ from West Africa, East Africa or Europe. Each zone publishes its own parameters under the same framework.

This is why there is no single price list in this documentation. The framework is the constant; the zone parameters are the variables.

## The published split

Every contract follows the same allocation:

| Allocation | Share |
| --- | --- |
| Developer | 40%, sacred |
| Success Delivery Manager | 8 to 12%, complexity-based |
| Marketing | 12%, organic-first |
| Acquisition agent | 3 to 7%, base + commission + retention |
| Founder | 3%, scales with revenue |
| Tech infrastructure | 5% |
| Reserves | 5% |
| Remainder | reinvested in the network and operations |

The split is published here, in the documentation, and is the same for every client in every zone.

## Simulate your case

Because the framework is numeric, it can be simulated. The [simulator](/en/simulator) lets a decision maker:

- Enter a monthly contract value in FCFA or EUR
- Adjust the variable shares (SDM and agent) within their published ranges
- See the split update in real time as a chart
- Switch between the view of every actor: company, developer, SDM, agent, state

The simulator implements the published framework exactly. It is a decision tool, not a sales pitch.

## Zone parameters

Each market zone maintains its own set of parameters, under the same framework. Typical zone parameters include:

- Reference hourly rates by seniority level (e.g. Junior, Middle, Senior)
- Currency conventions (FCFA zones, EUR zones, other monetary areas)
- Local cost-of-living adjustments for the developer share baseline
- SLA variants adapted to local conditions, never below the framework minimum
- Legal and contract templates aligned with local law

Zone parameters are published and versioned like the framework itself. See [market zones and standards](/en/model/08-market-zones-and-standards) for the full explanation.

## What the price includes

When an adopter pays for a contract, they pay for a delivery system, not a headcount:

- The developer (40% of every contract)
- The Success Delivery Manager
- Tooling (Discord, GitHub, Cal.com, Formbricks)
- Quality gates and reporting
- The 72-hour replacement standard

## The honest no

The model maintains a minimum margin on all custom projects. If a budget cannot support fair developer pay and a healthy delivery system, the honest answer is given rather than squeezing the developer. This is why the model works for everyone, or not at all.
