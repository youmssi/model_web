The MRVIN100 model is a framework, and a framework must be stable where it matters and adaptable where reality differs. That is the role of market zones: the universal rules stay universal, and the local parameters are maintained per zone, transparently and publicly.

## Why zones exist

A company in Central Africa, a state in West Africa and a client in Europe do not share the same currency, cost of living, legal system or talent pool. Forcing one absolute price list on all of them would either price out local adopters or underpay developers.

The model solves this by separating two things:

- **The framework:** percentages, roles, guarantees and criteria. One set, published, valid everywhere.
- **Zone standards:** absolute prices, currency conventions, legal templates, SLA variants and certification criteria. Maintained per zone.

## The framework, constant everywhere

These do not change from zone to zone:

- 40% of every contract goes to the developer
- SDM share within 8 to 12%, tied to complexity
- Agent share within 3 to 7%
- The 72-hour replacement standard
- The five-step vetting process
- The SDM five-project cap
- The membership criteria

## Zone standards, maintained per zone

Each zone publishes and maintains its own set of parameters under the framework:

| Parameter | Example of variation |
| --- | --- |
| Reference hourly rates by level | Central Africa vs West Africa vs Europe |
| Currency conventions | FCFA zones, EUR zones, other monetary areas |
| Cost-of-living adjustments | baseline for the developer share |
| SLA variants | never below the framework minimum |
| Legal and contract templates | aligned with local law |
| Certification criteria | adapted to local education systems |

A parameter in the framework cannot be changed by a zone. A zone standard can only change through the zone's published maintenance process.

## How standards are maintained

Standards are living documents. The maintenance process is public:

1. **Proposal.** A change to a zone standard is proposed with a rationale and data.
2. **Review.** Adopters of the zone are consulted; the developer pact is never weakened.
3. **Versioning.** The change is published with a version number, a date and the previous value.
4. **Migration.** Current contracts keep their signed terms; new contracts use the new standard.

Nothing changes silently. Every update is visible in this documentation and in the zone's published standards.

## Zone examples

To illustrate, two zones might publish different reference rates for the same seniority level, because the cost bases differ:

| Seniority | Zone A (Central Africa) | Zone B (West Africa) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Junior | 15,000 FCFA / h | 18,000 FCFA / h |
| Middle | 25,000 FCFA / h | 30,000 FCFA / h |
| Senior | 40,000 FCFA / h | 48,000 FCFA / h |

The percentages stay identical. The absolute values adapt. Both are published, so everyone can verify both.

## Why this matters for adopters

- **Predictability:** you know which rules are universal and which are local
- **Fairness:** zones cannot exploit their position to underpay developers
- **Transparency:** every standard, every version, is public
- **Scale:** the model can expand across the continent without losing its principles
