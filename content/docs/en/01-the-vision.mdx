MRVIN100 is an open model for engineering in Africa. It is a published framework of standards, not a closed company selling services. Any company, developer, state or institution can adopt it, and the standards are maintained publicly so everyone can verify them.

This documentation explains the model in full: how it works, how money flows, how it is governed, and how anyone can join.

## The problem the model answers

For years, African enterprises and institutions have been underserved in four ways:

1. **Distance in time, not just geography.** Teams in different timezones turn a 10-minute question into a 24-hour round-trip.
2. **Opaque pricing.** Intermediaries take 50 to 70% of the contract value without ever publishing their margin.
3. **No accountability.** When a developer underperforms, there is no guarantee, no replacement, no recourse.
4. **Brain drain.** The best African engineers are recruited abroad, and the continent never builds its own capacity.

MRVIN100 was designed as a direct answer to all four.

## The answer: a framework, not a service catalog

Adopting the model does not mean "buying a freelancer." It means plugging into a complete delivery system defined by published standards:

- **A vetted talent network** of Cameroonian engineers, selected through a five-step process.
- **A Success Delivery Manager (SDM)** who coordinates sprints, reports and quality gates on every project.
- **Published economics** where the revenue split is public, and 40% of every contract goes to the developer.
- **A 72-hour replacement standard** on every contract, with no exceptions and no extra invoice.

This is the difference between renting a person and subscribing to a delivery capability.

## The three commitments

Every standard in the model traces back to three commitments:

### 1. Radical transparency

The revenue split is published in this documentation. The developer's share of 40% is non-negotiable. Clients know exactly what they pay for, and developers know exactly what they earn. No hidden margin, no surprises.

### 2. Accountable delivery

A Success Delivery Manager owns coordination and quality on every project, capped at five projects at a time. Delivery quality is someone's job, not an aspiration.

### 3. The 72-hour standard

If a developer underperforms or becomes unavailable, a replacement profile is presented within 72 hours, with a paid knowledge-transfer overlap. The project never stalls. This is contractual, not aspirational.

## An open collective

The model is designed to be adopted, not owned. Companies adopt it for their engineering. Developers adopt it for their career. States and institutions adopt it to structure national ecosystems. Each market zone maintains its own standards under the same published framework, so adoption scales across the continent without diluting the principles.

- **For companies:** a predictable, accountable engineering team, with numbers you can verify.
- **For developers:** a career path that pays fairly and builds continental capacity.
- **For states and institutions:** a playbook for structuring national tech ecosystems.
