MRVIN100 is an open framework for organizing and growing African engineering talent. The model is about people, engineering work and the conditions that help capability grow. Software tools may help people apply the framework, but a software product is not the model itself.

## The premise

African engineers can build world-class products and systems. The central challenge is not an absence of talent. It is that capable engineers and employers do not always meet through arrangements that provide the support, context, pay, delivery practices and room to grow that good work needs.

Many African organizations have relied on offshore suppliers to access engineering capacity. That can bring expertise, but may also add layers of cost, time-zone friction, unclear responsibility or distance from local needs. These problems vary by supplier and contract; they are not a judgment about engineers outside Africa. The question is how more organizations can work directly with excellent African engineers under clear and sustainable conditions.

## The founding question

A project experience prompted a simple question: when an organization in Africa pays heavily for a technology project, why can part of the work end up being done by African engineers only after the project has travelled through a distant provider?

That experience is a founding observation, not a market statistic. It helped shape a wider ambition: keep more engineering opportunity, learning and economic value connected to the places and people where the work matters.

## What MRVIN100 proposes

The model organizes engineering work around country-aware pay benchmarks, transparent pricing assumptions, clear delivery responsibilities, sound engineering practice, and mentorship that helps early-career engineers grow. It aims to make cross-border work within Africa easier to evaluate and to adapt the framework to the law, currency, labor market and operating conditions of each country.

The target is not the highest possible margin. It is sustainable, high-quality work that can broaden local engineering capacity before demand outpaces it.

As that capacity grows, more experienced engineers and stronger local organizations can also make it easier to build products for African consumers and businesses. This is a long-term opportunity, not a promised outcome; it depends on talent, capital, infrastructure, customer trust and supportive country conditions.

## What the model is not

MRVIN100 is not a staffing marketplace, a promise that every project will be cheaper, or a fixed continental price list. It does not guarantee that money will never leave a country, and it does not ask employers to choose a team only because it is African. Quality, fit, security, scope and accountability remain essential.

The framework is open to scrutiny. Its evidence, assumptions, country adaptations, limitations and failures should be visible so practitioners can improve it.
