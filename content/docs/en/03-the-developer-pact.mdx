Engineers are the foundation of the model. MRVIN100 starts from the belief that African engineering talent can meet world-class standards and deserves working conditions that make strong, sustainable work possible.

## Fair compensation grounded in local evidence

The model’s stated direction is to target pay within the upper 30 percent of comparable salaries in each country. This is a principle to validate with reliable, recent data by role and experience. It is not a published salary guarantee or a claim that the right figure is already known for every market.

A country benchmark should identify its source, date, sample, job family, seniority and limitations. Where evidence is weak or incomparable, the uncertainty should be shown rather than hidden behind a single precise number.

## Clear professional agreements

Before work begins, engineers and organizations should agree on the role, scope, rate or salary, payment timing, working hours, leave, equipment, intellectual property, confidentiality, security, review process and exit or handover expectations. Applicable labor and tax law takes precedence over general model guidance.

No one should be asked to absorb unpaid scope growth, indefinite availability or unclear payment risk. A fair agreement is understandable to both the person doing the work and the organization receiving it.

## Mentorship that has time behind it

Growing the talent pool requires learning through real engineering work. Senior and mid-level engineers can mentor early-career colleagues through pairing, code review, design discussion and gradual ownership. A starting planning idea is one experienced engineer supporting up to two juniors, but the ratio must be adapted to workload, skill level and the actual time available. It is not a mandatory quota.

Mentorship should be recognized as work, planned into delivery and reviewed for whether the junior engineer is gaining useful responsibility.

## Voice, safety and growth

Engineers should be able to raise quality concerns, question unsafe deadlines and give feedback without retaliation. Career progression should be based on demonstrated skills and contribution, with access to meaningful projects and learning.

The framework is incomplete wherever local engineers cannot inspect, challenge or improve its pay assumptions and working rules.
