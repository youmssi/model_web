title: Governance and transparency
description: How an open framework can earn trust through clear status, evidence and accountable change.

An open framework earns trust when people can see how its rules are made, who maintains them, what evidence supports them and how to challenge them.

## Separate principle, proposal and adopted rule

Every statement should have a clear status:

- **Core principle:** the purpose or value the framework is built around.
- **Proposal:** a specific rule under discussion or testing.
- **Adopted guidance:** a published practice approved through the documented governance process.
- **Country rule:** a local adaptation reviewed against the relevant law and evidence.
- **Observed result:** a measured outcome with a stated method and source.

A proposal must not be written as a contract promise. A source from one country must not be presented as an African average.

## Publish the record of change

A future governance process should publish the framework version, maintainers, decision method, feedback period, change log and unresolved objections. Country annexes should identify who contributed, when evidence was checked and when it should be reviewed again.

## Disclose interests and limitations

Anyone maintaining a rule or reviewing an adopter should disclose relevant commercial or institutional interests. Reviews should distinguish self-reported information from independently checked evidence. Corrections and disagreements should be recorded rather than erased.

## Current status

MRVIN100 is still defining its stewardship and public recognition process. This documentation therefore does not claim independent certification, a completed adopter registry or a final legal governance structure. Those mechanisms should be designed with the people expected to rely on them.
