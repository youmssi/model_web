The model works in two layers: the framework itself, which is public and constant, and the engagement process, which applies it to a specific company. This chapter covers both.

## Layer 1: the published framework

Before any contract, the framework is already public. Anyone can verify:

- The revenue split, including the 40% developer share
- The SDM role and the five-project cap
- The 72-hour replacement standard
- The membership criteria and the joining process

This documentation is the framework. It is versioned and updated transparently per market zone, so adopters always build against the current standard.

## Layer 2: the engagement process

When a company adopts the model, the engagement follows four steps:

### Step 1. Tell us your needs

You share your project, your stack and your timeline. This can be as short as a discovery call or as detailed as a written brief.

Your request is qualified within 72 hours, and you are told honestly whether the network can staff it. If the niche or the seniority cannot be matched, we say so before you invest time or money.

### Step 2. We match your team

The right profiles are selected from the vetted, Cameroon-based network. Matching criteria include:

- Technical fit with your stack (React, Next.js, Node.js, Python, PostgreSQL, DevOps, mobile)
- Seniority level (Junior, Middle, Senior)
- Communication style and English/French fluency
- Availability and timezone alignment (CET/WAT)

You review the proposed profiles before any contract is signed.

### Step 3. Your SDM takes over

A Success Delivery Manager is assigned to your project. They handle:

- Daily coordination with your developers
- Sprint planning and backlog hygiene
- Weekly progress reports
- Escalation of blockers in your language
- Quality gates before every delivery

One SDM manages at most five projects at a time. Quality is capped by design.

### Step 4. Deliver with confidence

Delivery runs on a fixed cadence:

- Weekly progress reports
- Bi-weekly stakeholder demos
- Two-week sprints with retrospectives
- 72-hour replacement standard on every contract

You always know what was done, what is next, and what is blocked.

## The tooling

The model standardizes on a small, battle-tested stack that clients already know:

| Tool | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| **Discord** | Daily communication and standups |
| **GitHub** | Code, pull requests and reviews |
| **Google Meet** | Demos and stakeholder calls |
| **Cal.com** | Scheduling, no back-and-forth emails |
| **Formbricks** | Feedback, surveys and intake forms |

If your organization has its own tooling (Jira, Slack, Linear), the model adapts. The defaults exist to keep cost and friction low.

## Part-time to full-time

Most clients start part-time (80h/month per developer) to validate the collaboration, then scale to full-time as needs grow. Your developer, your SDM and your tooling stay identical, only the hours change.

## What this means for you

- **No procurement maze:** one intake, one contract, one point of contact.
- **No black box:** weekly reports and bi-weekly demos are contractual, not optional.
- **No lock-in:** start part-time, scale up, or stop, month to month.
